# The Rich Man and Lazarus — Jeffery Smith

**Author:** Jeffery Smith (pastor)
**Publisher:** Evangelical Press (EP Books), 2019 · 96 pages
**Text:** Luke 16:19–31 (book quotes NKJV — wording differs slightly from ESV)
**Origin:** Edited sermons preached to his own congregation. Leans on Blanchard
(*Whatever Happened to Hell?*), Peterson (*Hell on Trial*), Ryle, and
especially Brownlow North's exposition of Luke 16:19–31.

## Summary

Smith reads the story as **both history and parable**: Luke never calls it a
parable, it opens "there was…", and uniquely names a character (Lazarus) —
yet the unseen-world scenes (conversation across the gulf, finger, tongue,
water) are necessarily figurative. Either way, he insists the lessons stand.

- **Ch 1 — The Contrast:** The rich man (purple, fine linen, daily splendor)
  ignores the diseased beggar at his gate; both die and their states reverse
  instantly. Lazarus was not saved *by* poverty nor the rich man damned *by*
  wealth (Abraham, David, Joseph were rich and godly) — the issue is unbelief
  versus Spirit-wrought repentance and faith in Christ.
- **Ch 2 — The Place and the Pains:** Hell (Hades here = abode of the wicked,
  not merely the grave) is a real place of conscious torment: banishment from
  God's gracious presence and common grace, inward agonies (memory, awakened
  conscience, regret, despair), active wrath, and anticipation of bodily
  resurrection to worse.
- **Ch 3 — Prayer Too Late:** "Father Abraham, have mercy" appeals to fleshly
  covenant privilege; sons of the church without new birth will be in hell.
  Hell's prayers are misdirected (not to God), impenitent (relief, not
  repentance), and unanswered. True prayer is a felt heart-cry now.
- **Ch 4 — No Escape:** "Son, remember" plus the fixed gulf = finality.
  Eternity defended four ways: *aionios* used of God/Christ/Spirit/heaven;
  Matt 25:46's parallel (deny one, deny both); unquenched-fire language;
  moral necessity (sin against an infinite God; the damned keep sinning).
  Punishment varies in degree, not duration.
- **Ch 5 — The Scriptures:** "They have Moses and the prophets" — the Word is
  sufficient *and* the most effective means of salvation, Christ-centered,
  self-authenticating (needing no church/tradition/miracle to prove it), with
  the Spirit witnessing *by and with* the Word against willful suppression.
  Evangelism = explain and apply Scripture + prayer, not entertainment or
  pragmatism.

## Double-check while reading (author's own cautions)

- **Crumbs (Luke 16:21):** "we can't be dogmatic" that the rich man gave
  nothing — inference from omission + context.
- **Figurative vs literal:** finger, tongue, water, eyes, Abraham's bosom,
  conversation across the gulf — figures for spiritual realities; pressing
  them literally yields strange conclusions.
- **Hades/Sheol:** sometimes the general grave, sometimes punitive —
  Smith argues context here demands the latter (Ps 9:17; Prov 15:24;
  Matt 11:23–24). Worth testing.
- **"Their worm" (Mark 9:48) = conscience:** presented as "very likely,"
  not certain.
- **Degrees of punishment:** Edwards's infinite-line illustration; duration
  same, intensity differs — check the logic.
- **Rich man's motive as self-justification** (blaming God for insufficient
  warning): "the text does not specifically state it… I think."
- **Gehenna as Jerusalem sewage:** "according to some later sources, it
  *may have* become" — tentative.
- **Typo alert:** book prints "Ex. 33:9 / Rom. 9:15" for "no mercy owed" —
  should be **Ex. 33:19**.
- **Eternity from the gulf image alone:** Smith concedes it "might be
  argued" the image doesn't necessitate eternality — his case rests on the
  four arguments above. Test each.
