We see this idea with the sabbath as well: a time to work and a time to rest; there is a time for hard work and a time to care for the household. This is a different rhythm than what we have today: work all the time and not care for the house, meaning the building of the family values.

      And he said, “He who has ears to hear, let him hear.” (Mk 4:9)

Does this not mean everyone, I suppose in a literal sense who can hear, but I do not see that. I see who ever wants to hear, give him the space to hear. Whoever has the capacity to hear, meaning whoever has reason, that is willing to consider reasoning give him the ear to hear. 


      “ ‘they may indeed see but not perceive, 
      and may indeed hear but not understand, 
      lest they should turn and be forgiven.’ ” (Mk 4:12)

Based on the previous statement this seems logical and reasoning makes sense, we are to reason with the Gospel as we will find that it has complete authority and we bow our knee to the Lord, Jesus.

     Pay attention to what you hear: with the measure you use, it will be measured to you, and still more will be added to you. 
           25       For to the one who has, more will be given, and from the one who has not, even what he has will be taken away.” (Mk 4:24-25)

I have heard this passage used to cite financial freedom, but I get the sense that this is not financial but has to do with reason and faith. The reasoning for my consideration is the context of the conversation; before this, Jesus is talking about the seed of the Gospel. In the examples, the cares of the world and Satan affect the results of the seed in the soil to try choke out the Gospel from the soil. Then this line is said, making it seem that faith is the currency being measured here.
