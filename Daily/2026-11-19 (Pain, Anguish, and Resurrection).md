      And she went up and laid him on the bed of the man of God and shut the door behind him and went out. (2 Ki 4:21)
I find this part interesting; this means either they had a relationship with Elisha, meaning they looked after him often, which means they had easy access to him, or they dedicated a portion of their house to the will of God. Either way, this seems like a deliberate step the family took to include God's will in their lives. Something that we can learn from.

	And he said, “Why will you go to him today? It is neither new moon nor Sabbath.” She said, “All is well.” (2 Ki 4:23)
This response seems a lot like what we may often say: I will wait till Sunday to pray, or worship, or bring an offering. She replies with the right attitude to this thinking: I will do it now.

	Then the mother of the child said, “As the Lord lives and as you yourself live, I will not leave you.” So he arose and followed her. (2 Ki 4:30)
The persistence of the woman almost seems comical in a way, like something you see in a movie, however how many times should we have said this to God in moments of distress and worry, “God, I will not leave you”?

The events of the Shunammite woman and her son show that God is the God of the living and the dead; He brings us to life, and He controls when we return to Him. Our role and goal should be to praise God and do His will.

	But his servant said, “How can I set this before a hundred men?” So he repeated, “Give them to the men, that they may eat, for thus says the Lord, ‘They shall eat and have some left.’ ” 44 So he set it before them. And they ate and had some left, according to the word of the Lord. (2 Ki 4:43–44)
This is very much like when Jesus feeds the 5000 and the 4000, there is a need, and God fills the need through His grace. The gospel is such a wonderful thing, taking the time to read the bible as a whole lets us see the provision of God in all times and spaces.

	But Naaman was angry and went away, saying, “Behold, I thought that he would surely come out to me and stand and call upon the name of the Lord his God, and wave his hand over the place and cure the leper. 12 Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be clean?” So he turned and went away in a rage. 13 But his servants came near and said to him, “My father, it is a great word the prophet has spoken to you; will you not do it? Has he actually said to you, ‘Wash, and be clean’?” (2 Ki 5:11–13)
There are moments when what we want is not what we need, and in moments like that we need “mat carriers”, “5am buddies”, “fox hole buddies”, people that can call us out and tell us - Jesus says… 

	He went in and stood before his master, and Elisha said to him, “Where have you been, Gehazi?” And he said, “Your servant went nowhere.” 26 But he said to him, “Did not my heart go when the man turned from his chariot to meet you? Was it a time to accept money and garments, olive orchards and vineyards, sheep and oxen, male servants and female servants? 27 Therefore the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your descendants forever.” So he went out from his presence a leper, like snow (2 Ki 5:25–27)
This seems a lot like the betrayal of Judas and the result seems similar, we can see that this kind of behaviour needs to be addressed and monitored as we never know when we can be led astray.
