The phrase “the king knew her not” indicates that despite Abishag’s role caring for David and lying in his arms for warmth, they did not have sexual relations. ([1 Kings 1:1–4](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.11.1.1-11.1.4))

With Adonijah, we see internal turmoil and deception within the circle of the king; we see him wanting to take the reins of his life and claim leadership, but David had already promised Solomon to be the successor; we see this today when people try to force their way into spaces that God did not intend for them to be. I need to be careful not to fall for the lies of self-worship that drive this behaviour.

Verses 11 to 27 can look like they are “ratting out” Adonijah, but actually it is fighting for what is right, as God put order into things, and here we can see a man trying to elevate himself above the order of God. As men, we are to push back darkness, not because God can’t, but because He has entrusted us to do it.

David has not always acted swiftly in the past, but this time he moves with a pace that suggests he knows he is at the end and hands over the kingdom to Solomon, as he said he would. As men, we need to honor our word and act when it is needed.
