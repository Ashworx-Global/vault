	And he arose and ate and drank, and went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights to Horeb, the mount of God. (1 Ki 19:8)
God provided the means; He deliberately knew that he needed the energy to act His will and provided food and water. The land was just coming out of a drought, so there would not be much food yet; in the process of the land being revived, God knew that Elijah still needed food, and He provided. God knows our need; we still need to do His work to our benefit.

God is so intentional in our lives and in the bible. This event is so important in Jewish culture as David entered in the front gates, and the entrance that Jesus come in from is the entrance for sacrifices, pointing to the sacrifice that will be given for all. God plans and executes things in His design, and to think other wise is foolish; even though we do not and cannot see the design, faith allows us to walk in situations even though we do not know why.

	And whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone, so that your Father also who is in heaven may forgive you your trespasses.” (Mk 11:25)
Forgiveness is such a miss understood and miss used term in our culture, yet it is so vital that we do it from a biblical perspective. From what I have read and understood, forgiveness is a state of the heart, the emotional and logical stance that you have against someone. When I forgive you, I quench the emotional perspective and logically release the animosity against you; however, it does not necessarily mean that the bond that was broken is mended at that moment, as there can be moments when a healing period can be required, or even a choice not to allow that bond in the first place. It still means the emotional weight and logical connection are released, giving me space to reconnect with the Father and reorient my heart to Him. I suspect this is why Jesus instructs us to forgive first, as then our heart is open to the Father, as the Father is open to us.

      So they answered Jesus, “We do not know.” And Jesus said to them, “Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.” (Mk 11:33)
I suspect that Jesus was pointing out where their hearts were pointing to; pride seemed to drive many of the decisions of the chief priests, as in the way they thought, what would people say, if that's the focus, then you cannot hear from God, as the voices in the head are the wrong ones.
