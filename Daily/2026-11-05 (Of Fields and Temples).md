	and he set up the pillar on the north and called its name Boaz. (1 Ki 7:21)

*The name Boaz carries two distinct meanings depending on context. When referring to the pillar in Solomon’s temple, Boaz means “strength.”[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D1727040%3A126%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A14.0.40%7CResourceVersion%3D2023-07-20T02%3A03%3A08Z) The name can also be understood as “it is strong” or “by him he is mighty,”[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D421873%3A122%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOlJFQzExS0kxIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxNS0xMC0yN1QwMDozODoxOVoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiNDIxODczIiwiaW5kZXhlZExlbmd0aCI6IjEyMiIsIm5iZiI6MTc5NDI4MTg3MSwiZXhwIjoxNzk0Mjg1NDcxLCJpYXQiOjE3OTQyODE4NzEsImlzcyI6IlRleHRSYW5nZUFjY2Vzc1Rva2VuIiwiYXVkIjoiMTM5MTYxNzIifQ.09AhFCx1CzRVRL7cYQk6N90MF_bgLDwQwgwO7jAZO7s%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AREC11KI1%7CResourceVersion%3D2015-10-27T00%3A38%3A19Z) conveying the solidity and permanence of the massive bronze structure that stood at the temple entrance.*

*The pillar itself held symbolic weight in Israel’s religious life. The biblical figure Boaz married Ruth and became the great-grandfather of King David,[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D421717%3A156%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOlJFQzExS0kxIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxNS0xMC0yN1QwMDozODoxOVoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiNDIxNzE3IiwiaW5kZXhlZExlbmd0aCI6IjE1NiIsIm5iZiI6MTc5NDI4MTg3MiwiZXhwIjoxNzk0Mjg1NDcyLCJpYXQiOjE3OTQyODE4NzIsImlzcyI6IlRleHRSYW5nZUFjY2Vzc1Rva2VuIiwiYXVkIjoiMTM5MTYxNzIifQ.UkTbLyM_CIP4KGQ7vklpOpEphPsII-fltX0wM9MBdfg%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AREC11KI1%7CResourceVersion%3D2015-10-27T00%3A38%3A19Z) so the pillar’s name likely evoked this connection to David’s royal lineage. [Psalm 21](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.19.21) begins and ends with “boaz,” using the same word for “strength” that Solomon employed to name his pillar,[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D422301%3A171%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOlJFQzExS0kxIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxNS0xMC0yN1QwMDozODoxOVoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiNDIyMzAxIiwiaW5kZXhlZExlbmd0aCI6IjE3MSIsIm5iZiI6MTc5NDI4MTg3MywiZXhwIjoxNzk0Mjg1NDczLCJpYXQiOjE3OTQyODE4NzMsImlzcyI6IlRleHRSYW5nZUFjY2Vzc1Rva2VuIiwiYXVkIjoiMTM5MTYxNzIifQ.NHBtI7q2A5zLhRyL-JXgFfmIIhvj1ayhRjp16cXjoss%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AREC11KI1%7CResourceVersion%3D2015-10-27T00%3A38%3A19Z) suggesting the name was chosen to resonate with Israel’s worship and royal theology.*

The detail that Solomon had in creating the temple and everything around it, for me shows how much care and consideration we should take in our daily lives as what we do should be offerings for the Father and out of love for Jesus for all that He has done. Half-baked efforts are not enough.

	And leaving the crowd, they took him with them in the boat, just as he was. (Mk 4:36)

The phrase “just as he was” captures a specific moment of exhaustion. Interpreters have generally understood this expression to mean “immediately” or “without returning to shore,”[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D869018%3A164%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkJFQ05UNjJNSyIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTItMDItMTNUMjI6MDc6MDVaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6Ijg2OTAxOCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNjQiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQyODI2MzMsImV4cCI6MTc5NDI4NjIzMywiaWF0IjoxNzk0MjgyNjMzLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.NY-qJN-HO-_hmbqaPpU_sGtLlNrXhNoO44hTXHIJJsc%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ABECNT62MK%7CResourceVersion%3D2012-02-13T22%3A07%3A05Z) emphasizing the urgency of the departure rather than any delay.

However, there’s a deeper human dimension at play. The disciples observed that Jesus was exhausted, a conclusion confirmed by his ability to sleep soundly through the approaching storm.[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D203126%3A133%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkZSTFNTSFdDVlJZVEhORyIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMjUtMDYtMTlUMjE6MTg6MTVaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjIwMzEyNiIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxMzMiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQyODI2MzQsImV4cCI6MTc5NDI4NjIzNCwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MjgyNjM0LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.aZMa2L3VJcQCvujEruT0lRAGjBPRHKcfGRuARlSrBWI%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AFRLSSHWCVRYTHNG%7CResourceVersion%3D2025-06-19T21%3A18%3A15Z) Rather than waiting for Jesus to recover or prepare himself, they simply took him aboard in his current state of fatigue. This detail reveals Jesus’s full humanity—he was too tired to continue, so the disciples made a transportation decision for him.[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D203299%3A173%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkZSTFNTSFdDVlJZVEhORyIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMjUtMDYtMTlUMjE6MTg6MTVaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjIwMzI5OSIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNzMiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQyODI2MzUsImV4cCI6MTc5NDI4NjIzNSwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MjgyNjM1LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.Yx1mZIA4qLaZXdlx9u_onGFITTbE8nI_jOCfMO3TpZU%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AFRLSSHWCVRYTHNG%7CResourceVersion%3D2025-06-19T21%3A18%3A15Z)

The phrasing is admittedly unusual because it reverses the expected dynamic. Typically in Mark’s Gospel, Jesus is described as “taking” the disciples or others with him, rather than the reverse as occurs here.[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D868840%3A178%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkJFQ05UNjJNSyIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTItMDItMTNUMjI6MDc6MDVaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6Ijg2ODg0MCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNzgiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQyODI2MzUsImV4cCI6MTc5NDI4NjIzNSwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MjgyNjM1LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.xWIl4fRH0RRy3OMQVuPc-ZoPSE3ckrRLOu7DBlgk4Q8%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ABECNT62MK%7CResourceVersion%3D2012-02-13T22%3A07%3A05Z) This inversion underscores the disciples’ protective concern for their exhausted teacher. They didn’t wait for his instruction; they acted with practical compassion, recognizing his need for rest even as they embarked into the night.

So the oddness of the wording serves Mark’s purpose: it highlights both the immediacy of their departure and the very human vulnerability of Jesus in that moment—tired, dependent on his followers’ care, yet about to demonstrate authority over nature itself.

     He said to them, “Why are you rso afraid? Have you still no faith?” 
           41       And they were filled with great fear and said to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?” (Mk 4:40-41)

In hindsight, we know that Jesus is Lord. He is the creator of everything, so if He is the creator of the very thing that makes wind possible, then this is rather easy for Jesus, but the disciples have not come to the realisation yet; the troubles of life were in front of them, as that is what they know. How often do we get in front of problems, and that is all that can be seen; however, the maker of everything, even of the issue that is at hand, can calm that very issue.

	And when Jesus had stepped out of the boat, immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit. (Mk 5:2)

A miracle happened and in comes a demon, when good often happens and the enemy will want to change your focus away from Him and focus on problems.

      And crying out with a loud voice, he said, “What have you to do with me, Jesus, ySon of the Most High God? aI adjure you by God, do not torment me.” (Mk 5:7)


Satan believes in God, demons believe in God, believing in God is not enough, God must be the Lord of your life, that is what it is to be a Christian. To bow my knee to Jesus is to be a follower of Jesus, not sprinkle Jesus on my life but to live for Him as Lord of my life.
