The Feast of Booths occurs on the fifteenth day of the seventh month and lasts seven days ([Lev 23:33–43](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.3.23.33-3.23.43)), with an eighth day of solemn assembly following ([Lev 23:33–43](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.3.23.33-3.23.43)). This timing falls in late September to late October, after the completion of grain threshing and grape pressing[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D3908226%3A176%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ALBD%7CResourceVersion%3D2025-04-29T00%3A09%3A20Z).

The festival serves dual purposes. Exodus emphasizes its agricultural significance as the “Feast of Ingathering,” celebrating thanks giving and joy following the harvest and God’s abundant provision[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D250681%3A300%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkhOREJLSldTSENIUkZUSCIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMjAtMDEtMThUMDE6MDg6MTNaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjI1MDY4MSIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIzMDAiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTA1NjI1MzYsImV4cCI6MTc5MDU2NjEzNiwiaWF0IjoxNzkwNTYyNTM2LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.Y43nSh4Smo6AtFtZiWy-CUaw52So_yB6gzgq91GuGqY%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AHNDBKJWSHCHRFTH%7CResourceVersion%3D2020-01-18T01%3A08%3A13Z). More profoundly, participants dwell in booths for seven days to remember that God made the people of Israel dwell in booths during their exodus from Egypt ([Lev 23:33–43](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.3.23.33-3.23.43)). This week-long celebration commemorates the forty-year wilderness journey[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D14927%3A525%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjk3ODE1OTYzNjU0MTQiLCJyZXNvdXJjZVZlcnNpb24iOiIyMDIzLTA5LTA5VDA4OjUwOjAwWiIsImluZGV4ZWRPZmZzZXQiOiIxNDkyNyIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiI1MjUiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTA1NjI1MzcsImV4cCI6MTc5MDU2NjEzNywiaWF0IjoxNzkwNTYyNTM3LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.g2azghUdsCVcl5NXlisWvoofqI2Cjyxt7jSLpBQWVZ4%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A9781596365414%7CResourceVersion%3D2023-09-09T08%3A50%3A00Z), serving as a reminder of God’s faithfulness and protection[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D15462%3A68%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjk3ODE1OTYzNjU0MTQiLCJyZXNvdXJjZVZlcnNpb24iOiIyMDIzLTA5LTA5VDA4OjUwOjAwWiIsImluZGV4ZWRPZmZzZXQiOiIxNTQ2MiIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiI2OCIsIm5iZiI6MTc5MDU2MjUzNywiZXhwIjoxNzkwNTY2MTM3LCJpYXQiOjE3OTA1NjI1MzcsImlzcyI6IlRleHRSYW5nZUFjY2Vzc1Rva2VuIiwiYXVkIjoiMTM5MTYxNzIifQ.JPAjLyJxjA7K74EQRMiwpAW93zBuHvmNthcI8St32IA%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A9781596365414%7CResourceVersion%3D2023-09-09T08%3A50%3A00Z).

On the first day, participants gather fruit of splendid trees, palm branches, leafy boughs, and willows, and rejoice before the LORD for seven days ([Lev 23:33–43](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.3.23.33-3.23.43)). The festival was one of three pilgrimage feasts when all Jewish males were required to travel to Jerusalem to “appear before the Lord”[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D15530%3A143%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjk3ODE1OTYzNjU0MTQiLCJyZXNvdXJjZVZlcnNpb24iOiIyMDIzLTA5LTA5VDA4OjUwOjAwWiIsImluZGV4ZWRPZmZzZXQiOiIxNTUzMCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNDMiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTA1NjI1MzksImV4cCI6MTc5MDU2NjEzOSwiaWF0IjoxNzkwNTYyNTM5LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0._0lN54OfsfmsXh930PQWIE3rwWs_xMt3Kcymt7phgdg%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A9781596365414%7CResourceVersion%3D2023-09-09T08%3A50%3A00Z).

The feast carries eschatological significance. Prophetic promises of the Lord’s return to dwell among his people with the establishment of his kingdom on earth were naturally linked with this feast, with fulfilment predicted in [Zechariah 14](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.38.14)[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D252194%3A156%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkhOREJLSldTSENIUkZUSCIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMjAtMDEtMThUMDE6MDg6MTNaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjI1MjE5NCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNTYiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTA1NjI1NDAsImV4cCI6MTc5MDU2NjE0MCwiaWF0IjoxNzkwNTYyNTQwLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.Kc_vu0PG9qt202RDSZgiS6LLOQ2tN7kmGzplmO4xfjA%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AHNDBKJWSHCHRFTH%7CResourceVersion%3D2020-01-18T01%3A08%3A13Z). In that future day, everyone who survives of all nations that came against Jerusalem shall go up year after year to worship the King and keep the Feast of Booths.

The remaining biblical passages referenced to include Deuteronomy’s account emphasizing rejoicing with family members and servants at the place the Lord chooses, with the promise of blessing in all produce and work ([Deut 16:13–15](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.5.16.13-5.16.15)), and Nehemiah’s description of the post-exilic celebration when the people constructed booths on roofs and in courtyards, experiencing great rejoicing after centuries of discontinuance ([Neh 8:13–18](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.16.8.13-16.8.18)).

Keeping it true, Paul presents to everyone who can hear, his testimony. We should keep our words simple and true, saying nonsense things are for children that have not experienced life. God is king ,and He is Lord, to speak otherwise is simply foolish. Times can call for plain words and expressing the, true does not need to sound like a poem for boastfully say words that have no substance, keeping it clear and simple is the best, as Paul does here. Lord give me clarity of mind to speak plainly.
