## Daily prayer

Father God, thank you for your love and care; you are so gracious to me. I pray that my dear friends experience your grace and peace, that you bless their business, and that your overflowing abundance flows through them. I pray for my family and parents; your grace is sufficient for us. Thank you for a loving wife; I ask that you bless her and care for her. Thank you for my children that you have entrusted to me; I ask for wisdom that I may be a good father to them. I ask, Father God, for your wisdom and a heart for You; Holy Spirit, teach me as I read your Word. I ask this in Jesus' name.

The Greek word used in [Mark 3:29](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.62.3.29) is _blasphēméō_ (βλασφημέω), derived from _blásphēmos_[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D1097470%3A134%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjQ2LjEwLjEzIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxOS0xMS0wOFQwMTowNjoxMFoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiMTA5NzQ3MCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxMzQiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQxMzc2NTQsImV4cCI6MTc5NDE0MTI1NCwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MTM3NjU0LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.X3TIFwMsYQXEqM1p5AHcXIGbNJ9GpJRwexehmh711wM%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A46.10.13%7CResourceVersion%3D2019-11-08T01%3A06%3A10Z). This term combines two Greek words—_blaptō_ (“harm,” “damage”) and _phēmē_ (“reputation”)[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D59828%3A164%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkhBUlJJU05BVklHQVRJTkciLCJyZXNvdXJjZVZlcnNpb24iOiIyMDIwLTA2LTAyVDIyOjAzOjA1WiIsImluZGV4ZWRPZmZzZXQiOiI1OTgyOCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNjQiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQxMzc2NTQsImV4cCI6MTc5NDE0MTI1NCwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MTM3NjU0LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.x_IX8X2RC9zwXO98E3xMC99OuJE29hvxrn6IMhE5QlQ%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AHARRISNAVIGATING%7CResourceVersion%3D2020-06-02T22%3A03%3A05Z), so fundamentally it means to damage someone’s standing through harmful speech.

In the New Testament context, the word carries two distinct layers of meaning. At its broadest, it means “to blaspheme, revile” or “to hurt the reputation or smite with reports or words, speak evil of, slander, rail”[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D1097604%3A178%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjQ2LjEwLjEzIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxOS0xMS0wOFQwMTowNjoxMFoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiMTA5NzYwNCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNzgiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQxMzc2NTUsImV4cCI6MTc5NDE0MTI1NSwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MTM3NjU1LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.sHB4zTliIeMYJmoEMWKmmDT57g8bQJ0uM39Tkvqa9aU%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A46.10.13%7CResourceVersion%3D2019-11-08T01%3A06%3A10Z). More specifically, it refers to speaking “with impious irreverence concerning God Himself or what stands in some particular relation to Him”[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D1097946%3A151%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjQ2LjEwLjEzIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxOS0xMS0wOFQwMTowNjoxMFoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiMTA5Nzk0NiIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNTEiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQxMzc2NTYsImV4cCI6MTc5NDE0MTI1NiwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MTM3NjU2LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.1fpr1TgB8psfjZf55JMqpiUqVASrzoIs-zsro577fs4%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A46.10.13%7CResourceVersion%3D2019-11-08T01%3A06%3A10Z).

The Old Testament background proves crucial for understanding Jesus’s usage. In the Old Testament the term was used for derisive language toward the God of the covenant, with the fundamental notion expressed in [Leviticus 24:15–16](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.3.24.15-3.24.16): “Whoever curses his God shall bear his sin. He who blasphemes the name of the Lord shall be put to death”[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D417186%3A598%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A42.110.32%7CResourceVersion%3D2018-09-14T21%3A00%3A22Z).

In [Mark 3:29](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.62.3.29) specifically, reviling against the Holy Spirit means to resist the convicting power of the Holy Spirit unto repentance[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D1098447%3A130%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOjQ2LjEwLjEzIiwicmVzb3VyY2VWZXJzaW9uIjoiMjAxOS0xMS0wOFQwMTowNjoxMFoiLCJpbmRleGVkT2Zmc2V0IjoiMTA5ODQ0NyIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxMzAiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTQxMzc2NTgsImV4cCI6MTc5NDE0MTI1OCwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MTM3NjU4LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.6JOO7z4TsVCTWrQFtnFnFSOE3rrep18uw8YBMYFvM7A%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A46.10.13%7CResourceVersion%3D2019-11-08T01%3A06%3A10Z). The scribes’ accusation that Jesus cast out demons through Beelzebul—rather than through God’s power—exemplified this sin. Jesus teaches that anyone who verbally attributes the works of the Spirit to Satan commits blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, a sin that cannot be forgiven because such slander reveals a hardened heart that has ultimately rejected the convicting work of the Holy Spirit[4](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D3785421%3A346%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ALBD%7CResourceVersion%3D2025-04-29T00%3A09%3A20Z).

      For whoever udoes the will of God, he is my brother and sister and mother.” (Mk 3:35)

This seems to indicate the greater “C” church, the unified union between culture and race, the unification of all under the Lordship of Jesus. We are uniquely and wonderfully made, and all will worship the King of Kings, Jesus.
