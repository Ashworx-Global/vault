#### FROM LOGOS:
In [Ezekiel 21:12](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.26.21.12), the command to “strike therefore upon your thigh” is a symbolic gesture expressing profound emotional anguish. Striking the thigh was a Jewish practice for expressing deep sorrow during mourning[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D647086%3A90%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A29.1.38%7CResourceVersion%3D2017-03-27T16%3A33%3A36Z), and Ezekiel was commanded to perform this action as a significant way of expressing the sorrow that was to come on rebellious Israel[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D647176%3A150%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A29.1.38%7CResourceVersion%3D2017-03-27T16%3A33%3A36Z).

The broader context intensifies this meaning. The prophecy announces judgment against God’s people and all the princes of Israel, who are delivered over to the sword ([Ezek 21:1–23](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.26.21.1-26.21.23)). Historically, this sword represented King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon and his armies[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D3877376%3A94%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ACSBSTUDYBIBLE%7CResourceVersion%3D2023-04-19T23%3A08%3A23Z). Rather than simply announcing judgment, God instructs Ezekiel to embody the lamentation—to physically demonstrate the devastation that awaits. Ezekiel is outwardly mourning for events that have not yet occurred[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D4728543%3A69%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AFSB%7CResourceVersion%3D2025-07-30T17%3A17%3A47Z), making his performed grief a prophetic sign to the people.

The verb used here can mean “to strike oneself” on the hip or thigh in abhorrence or grief[4](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D12174742%3A113%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOldTX0NPTUJJQkxJQkhFQiIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMjEtMDUtMDVUMTg6MDM6MTRaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjEyMTc0NzQyIiwiaW5kZXhlZExlbmd0aCI6IjExMyIsIm5iZiI6MTc5MTg1ODI1MiwiZXhwIjoxNzkxODYxODUyLCJpYXQiOjE3OTE4NTgyNTIsImlzcyI6IlRleHRSYW5nZUFjY2Vzc1Rva2VuIiwiYXVkIjoiMTM5MTYxNzIifQ.218XD4C4HnRN5jE3fSgUvlXEOwSWbHKrF_AIDFJ12RY%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AWS_COMBIBLIBHEB%7CResourceVersion%3D2021-05-05T18%3A03%3A14Z). This wasn’t merely an emotional outburst but a ritualised, recognisable gesture that communicated to observers the severity of what was coming. For many centuries the Greeks and Persians had a similar custom, as did many parts of the East[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D647326%3A96%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3A29.1.38%7CResourceVersion%3D2017-03-27T16%3A33%3A36Z), suggesting this was a widely understood expression of despair across ancient Near Eastern cultures.

The command sits within a series of dramatic prophetic actions where Ezekiel becomes a living message—the prophet was acting out his prophecy in some way[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D4729597%3A158%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AFSB%7CResourceVersion%3D2025-07-30T17%3A17%3A47Z)—transforming his own body into a warning about judgment and loss.

#### LOGOS SAID:
The concerns that make up our mini-narratives can sometimes distract us from the great drama in which we have been cast. When a mighty angel appears with a scroll in John’s revelation, the apostle’s part in God’s great redemptive drama suddenly becomes very clear. He swaps his role of scribe for that of actor, speaking God’s very words:

“And I went to the angel and told him to give me the little scroll, and he said to me, ‘Take and eat it up, and it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey.’ And I took the little scroll from the hand of the angel and ate it up, and it was sweet as honey in my mouth, and when I had eaten it, my stomach was made bitter. And they said to me, ‘It is necessary for you to prophesy again about many peoples and nations and languages and kings’ ” (Rev 10:9–11).

John’s new task parallels the prophet Ezekiel’s call to speak God’s words. The prophet eats a scroll to internalize and speak the words of Yahweh, which turn sweet in his mouth (Ezek 2:8–36; see Psa 119:103; Jer 15:16). …
 John D. Barry and Rebecca Kruyswijk, [_Connect the Testaments: A One-Year Daily Devotional with Bible Reading Plan_](https://ref.ly/logosres/fsbdevotional?ref=DayOfYear.Oct+10&off=90&ctx=10%3a1%E2%80%9311%3b+Job+35%3a1%E2%80%938%0a~The+concerns+that+ma) (Bellingham, WA: Lexham Press, 2012).
