	And they were seeking to arrest him but feared the people, for they perceived that he had told the parable against them. So they left him and went away. (Mk 12:12)
I find it interesting that they got a sense that Jesus was speaking or showing how the Pharisees would treat Jesus at the time of his execution, and how people will treat His name later and in the future. Truth cuts through all things, Pharisees like a sword, separating light and darkness, right and wrong.

	But, knowing their hypocrisy, he said to them, “Why put me to the test? Bring me a denarius and let me look at it.” (Mk 12:15)

The Greek word carrying weight in Jesus’ question is **πειράζω** (**peirazō**), which means “**to test**” or “**to tempt.**” This verb appears throughout Mark’s Gospel when religious opponents challenge Jesus, and it signals something far more serious than a casual inquiry.

The Pharisees and Herodians function as enemies of Jesus in Mark’s narrative, motivated by the same hostility that drives Satan—their goal is to end his mission and destroy him, By using **πειράζω**, Jesus exposes their question about taxes as something fundamentally deceptive. Jesus’ awareness of their hypocrisy and his exposure of their ruse through the question “Why do you test me?” reveals his power to see through their scheme.

The weight of this word becomes clearer when you recognize the pattern. Their tests involve setting verbal traps and seeking signs—actions that hint at their alliance with Satan, the cosmic tester. So when Jesus asks “**Why put me to the test?**” he’s not simply saying “**Why are you asking me this question?**” He’s identifying their entire approach as fundamentally adversarial, aligning them with demonic opposition to his mission.

The Greek word order itself emphasises this confrontation through a chiastic structure: “Why”, “me”, “do you test?” followed by Jesus’ countercommand to bring him a denarius. The question doesn’t invite dialogue—it exposes and rebukes. By naming their action **πειράζω**, Jesus transforms what appeared to be respectful flattery into what it truly was: a hostile attempt to trap him.

	Jesus said to them, “Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.” And they marveled at him. (Mk 12:17)
Seems Jesus is talking more about the position of he heart and what is lord over the heart; if Jesus is the Lord of your life, then everything that you do will be in service to the Father, so the things of eternal value- your praise, your testimony, your love- you render to the Father in praise and prayer. Your relationships you render to the Father through His word, as He said, love your neighbour, and care for one another. Do all things as if they are to the Father, and when we need to pay this world, do so.
