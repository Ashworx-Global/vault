Stand your ground: Elijah stood his ground. When the soldiers came more than once, he stood his ground, and God intervened, even though, to the natural eye, the situation didn't look good: fifty men came each time to sort him out, and God came through.  
God uses moments like this in our lives, moments that look like the odds are stacked against us; these moments are precious in our lives as they remind us how powerful God is. It will serve you well to write these moments down and remember them the next time things look dark.

           6:1       My son, if you have put up esecurity for your neighbor, 
      have given your pledge for a stranger, (Pr 6:1)
To put up security for your neighbor means to cosign or underwrite another person’s debt. 

You become a person who undertakes responsibility for a debt, or the fulfillment of an engagement by another.

In ancient practice, the striking of hands finalized the arrangement, probably performed in the presence of witnesses—similar to a handshake sealing a deal or signing a contract today.

The critical consequence is that people who pledge another’s debt become equally responsible for it; it becomes their debt as well. If the borrower defaults, you’re legally liable for the full amount. A pledge or security was collateral taken for a loan, and the one putting up security would be held responsible if the debtor defaulted on his loan.

Jesus did this with our sin, He took it on for Himself. Be carful not to forget or to treat it light heartly, and strive to keep it in your mind, as sin will try to make light of it and take you on a path where you should not want to be.
