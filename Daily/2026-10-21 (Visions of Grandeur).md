 And on all the walls all around, inside and outside, was a measured pattern (Eze 41:17)
 
God gives Ezekiel the images of the temple, in the scriptures the temple from what I have learnt so far is the picture of God’s desired relationship with His people, a place of order and closeness. How beautiful we can see how Jesus changed that reality and brought the Father even closer to us, we should still be in awe of His presence and His love, that for me is the order, but the case that we can bring our cares, and as the bible says “cast your cares, on Him for He cares for you”, brings a personal dimension to the reality that God wants and desires a relationship with us, with me, with you.

There they shall put the most holy offerings—the grain offering, the sin offering, and the guilt offering—for the place is holy. (Eze 42:13)

God is worthy of praise, and He makes sure that there is space for His worship, this is not because He needs it, but our benefit, that we can align our bent hearts to Him, so that we can receive His goodness. This is not a bad thing to offer gifts to God, but a way that He created to help us draw closer to Him.

Then another book was opened, which is the book of life. And the dead were judged by what was written in the books, according to what they had done. (Re 20:12)

Maybe this is where the idea of works based salvation comes from, lets dig a little deeper here.
The key term is _erga_ (works/deeds), which appears repeatedly in judgment passages like [Revelation 2:23](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.87.2.23) and 20:12[1](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D301550%3A176%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOkRDVFJOR0RQUlRUQ0hORyIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMjMtMDYtMTRUMTU6MTQ6NTVaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjMwMTU1MCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNzYiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTMwNDE5NTksImV4cCI6MTc5MzA0NTU1OSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMDQxOTU5LCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.OvtdHjdBh-ew6kmOb4F7F2jenhzAXRfbl29pajYa1DM%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ADCTRNGDPRTTCHNG%7CResourceVersion%3D2023-06-14T15%3A14%3A55Z).

Here’s the crucial distinction: _Erga_ refers to outward accomplishments that reflect inner motives—deeds with public evidence—rather than mere internal dispositions. These visible actions flow from a person’s deeper character and serve as the basis for divine judgment[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D766504%3A123%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOk1QUkVWRVhDT00wMSIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTYtMTEtMThUMTc6Mjg6MzhaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6Ijc2NjUwNCIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxMjMiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTMwNDE5NjAsImV4cCI6MTc5MzA0NTU2MCwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMDQxOTYwLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.3AUlDDvD3GRsg5r0ebtE7PDbp1O-WsDE8uEpkXZYJ08%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AMPREVEXCOM01%7CResourceVersion%3D2016-11-18T17%3A28%3A38Z). This is fundamentally different from the _works_ in “works-based salvation” debates, which typically refer to human effort attempting to earn favor.

The Greek phrase “kata ta erga autōn” (according to their works)[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D329305%3A296%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOlNMVlROTExHTkNMTiIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTctMDUtMzBUMjI6NTc6MjJaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjMyOTMwNSIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIyOTYiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTMwNDE5NjAsImV4cCI6MTc5MzA0NTU2MCwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMDQxOTYwLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.6HQA7KvVPtJHASb7upj0QrK0JN4Z6MwBhGeMG7H9Nmk%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ASLVTNLLGNCLN%7CResourceVersion%3D2017-05-30T22%3A57%3A22Z) appears throughout Scripture’s judgment passages. The principle “I will give to each one according to your works” (_dōsō hymin hekastǭ kata ta erga hymōn_) functions as Christ’s own judgment standard, stated multiple times across Revelation[2](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D765465%3A157%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOk1QUkVWRVhDT00wMSIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTYtMTEtMThUMTc6Mjg6MzhaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6Ijc2NTQ2NSIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIxNTciLCJuYmYiOjE3OTMwNDE5NjIsImV4cCI6MTc5MzA0NTU2MiwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMDQxOTYyLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.pgRzsDRPNTIcRdIUJGywRfD0oY1RavJvp4OQmYgc9jM%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3AMPREVEXCOM01%7CResourceVersion%3D2016-11-18T17%3A28%3A38Z). But notice: this measures _what you actually did_, not _how hard you tried_.

The distinction matters theologically. In Revelation’s vision, the final verdict examines both the books recording works and the Lamb’s book of life together—not as separate judgments of rewards versus eternal life[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D329817%3A468%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOlNMVlROTExHTkNMTiIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTctMDUtMzBUMjI6NTc6MjJaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjMyOTgxNyIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiI0NjgiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTMwNDE5NjIsImV4cCI6MTc5MzA0NTU2MiwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMDQxOTYyLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.2knG92hWBogh_CWgktBC_cR2I55UltnGJO_wA7BgH2c%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ASLVTNLLGNCLN%7CResourceVersion%3D2017-05-30T22%3A57%3A22Z). Paul addresses Christians specifically when stating that actual obedience—not merely intended obedience—is essential to final salvation[3](logos4-command:TextRangePreview%7CRanges%3D331609%3A252%3AeyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJyZXNvdXJjZUlkIjoiTExTOlNMVlROTExHTkNMTiIsInJlc291cmNlVmVyc2lvbiI6IjIwMTctMDUtMzBUMjI6NTc6MjJaIiwiaW5kZXhlZE9mZnNldCI6IjMzMTYwOSIsImluZGV4ZWRMZW5ndGgiOiIyNTIiLCJuYmYiOjE3OTMwNDE5NjMsImV4cCI6MTc5MzA0NTU2MywiaWF0IjoxNzkzMDQxOTYzLCJpc3MiOiJUZXh0UmFuZ2VBY2Nlc3NUb2tlbiIsImF1ZCI6IjEzOTE2MTcyIn0.TVc7fh8ml1GpEFdl88okfN3KqvKMIJ1bz8NeFm-t2uw%7CResourceId%3DLLS%3ASLVTNLLGNCLN%7CResourceVersion%3D2017-05-30T22%3A57%3A22Z). This reflects a different framework: your works reveal whether you’ve genuinely responded to Christ’s lordship, not whether you’ve accumulated enough merit points.
