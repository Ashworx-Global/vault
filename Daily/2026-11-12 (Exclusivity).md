The events leading up to Elijah's introduction show the hearts of men as they keep sinning and turning away from God. You see the chaos that happens when God is removed from the daily lives of men.

	And Elijah said to her, “Do not fear; go and do as you have said. But first make me a little cake of it and bring it to me, and afterward make something for yourself and your son. 1 Ki 17:13)
Jesus said fear not, this seems to be the expression of faith.

The woman whom Elijah stays with during this time, when her son was ill to the point of death, seems to lose her faith, even though provision is given miraculously, and she reaffirms her faith after her son is well. This seems to be a clear picture of what happens to us in a daily event, we are given God’s provision and we lose sight when something happens and require intervention to align our sight on Him again.

	Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to sin, it would be better for him if a great millstone were hung around his neck and he were thrown into the sea. (Mk 9:42)
Push back darkness; as men, as fathers, as leaders, we need to push back darkness. When we see darkness that causes sin to exist in spaces expecially for the children.

	For everyone will be salted with fire.(Mk 9:49)
I don’t think I have seen this before or recall it being pointed out before; Jesus was speaking of judgment and how to control the destination path by removing the part or aspect of sin that can cause judgment ending in Hell. Then Jesus changes perspectives; that seems to hinge on this line: judgment now is purification to remove the later judgment. I am not suggesting works-based faith, but through hardship and fire, the salt of our life, the potency of our testimony, is brought to life so that it can be useful, as mentioned in the following verse:
	Salt is good, but if the salt has lost its saltiness, how will you make it salty again? Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another.” (Mk 9:50)
