	Belteshazzar after the name of my god (Da 4:8)

Nebuchadnezzar stated that the name Belteshazzar was given “after the name of my god,” referring to the god Bel. However, the scholarly interpretation is more nuanced. While the king attributed the name to Bel, many scholars believe that the god’s name (probably Marduk) was actually omitted, and this verse reflects folk etymology based on an imprecise vocalization of the Babylonian form.

The name itself carries religious significance. The Babylonian form would be “Balatsu-usur” (meaning “protect his life”), or according to [Daniel 4:8](https://ref.ly/Da4.8), “Bel balatsu-usur.” Another scholarly rendering suggests the name may derive from “Belet/Belti-šar-uṣur,” meaning “May the Lady [wife of the god Bel] protect the king.”

What’s particularly interesting is that in recognition of the fact that Daniel’s God is the interpreter of his dream, Nebuchadnezzar called Daniel by his Hebrew name, the last syllable of which refers to Elohim, the God of Israel. This suggests that despite naming Daniel after his own Babylonian deity, the king recognized something distinctive about Daniel’s relationship with the God of Israel—a recognition that transcended the Babylonian naming convention.
