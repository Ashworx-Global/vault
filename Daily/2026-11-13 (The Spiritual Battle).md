From version 1 to 19, I see that Obadiah is acting like a person who says that they fear and follow God, but are more concerned with their own safety. At first, it is easy to judge him; however, do we not act like this at times? Sometimes scared to say something that goes against what others are saying? The Bible is such a good teacher, as when I read these events, and sit and think of what is happening, and to play it through my life, I get to see and the Holy Spirit shows, where there are areas of my life that need work. Praise be to God for His word and the clarity it brings.

      And Elijah came near to all the people and said, “How long will you go limping between two different opinions? If the LORD is God, follow him; but if Baal, then follow him.” And the people did not answer him a word (1 Ki 18:21)
Sometimes we need that guy, the one to call it like it is. We need that friend that can look us in the eye and say, are you going to follow Jesus or not?

     Fill four jars with water and pour it on the burnt offering and on the wood.” 
           34       And he said, “Do it a second time.” And they did it a second time. And he said, “Do it a third time.” And they did it a third time. 
           35       And the water ran around the altar and filled the trench also with water. (1 Ki 18:33-35)
There is still a drought, so to do this is not only to capture the greatness of God, but also to be an invitation to faith from those that were around him? Similar to the servants who did what Jesus said with the water-to-wine miracle, faith needed action, and Elijah brought those with him into the works of God by bringing the thing that was lacking in their lives to the altar of God.

Fire comes down; God burns the sacrifice and brings rain. These events can read like a story. Taking them to heart, the events and the impact they had on the lives of the people, we can get a sense of awe and understand the magnitude of what is happening here. Age and time have a way of doing this, forcing us to recollect and think of times when God has moved mountains for us, and at times without even thinking or asking. God is good.

It seems Jesus is getting to the heart of identity; Jesus knows who He is. It seems he is wanting people to understand what it means to acknowledge His identity, so as to not use it flippantly.

           And Jesus, looking at him, loved him, and said to him, “You lack one thing: go, sell all that you have and give to the poor, and you will have ctreasure in heaven; and come, follow me.” 
           22       Disheartened by the saying, he went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions (Mk 10:21-22)
The order of this seems too interesting to me. Jesus looked at him; Jesus looks at us, at me. Jesus wants a relationship with us. Jesus loves us before any instruction, before anything we do or say, before we send a WhatsApp to a group or send a word to a friend, before we have our morning prayer time, before we read our Bible, before anything and everything we can or will do; He loved us.

	And Jesus said to him, “What do you want me to do for you?” And the blind man said to him, “Rabbi, let me recover my sight.” (Mk 10:51)
Does not seem to be by chance that Jesus asks him what He should do for him, but it does seem that Jesus wants the man to declare his faith. We can learn from this: declare your faith by asking for the thing that needs divine intervention, and step into faith that God will provide.
